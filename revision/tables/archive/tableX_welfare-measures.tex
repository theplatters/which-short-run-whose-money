\textbf{Measure} & \textbf{What it measures} & \textbf{Faithful representation} & \textbf{How it treats public spending} \\
\midrule
Real GDP\newline \scriptsize (income-side)
& The wage bill $w\sum_i L_i$ deflated by a seven-component T\"ornqvist price index of final demand.
& Every closure, cell by cell; flat by construction in the full-employment mobile rows.
& Counts $g^G$ and the program as final demand --- it records output, not welfare. \\
Consumption\newline \scriptsize (T\"ornqvist)
& A T\"ornqvist quantity index of the household consumption bundle against its baseline.
& Wherever the basket grows proportionally --- which the consumption block does whenever prices are pinned. Elsewhere its gap to the exact measure is of higher order.
& Only through the household budget: a tax-financed program lowers $E^h$; the bundle itself is not valued. \\
Consumption\newline \scriptsize (exact money-metric)
& $E^h/\mathcal{P}(p)$, the change in real consumption expenditure. Exact to all orders for the CES household, and equal to the quantity index because $\mathcal{P}(p)=1$ is the numeraire.
& Every closure. It departs from the T\"ornqvist index only where the consumption basket leaves proportional growth --- in practice under the F1 preference shifter, where neither is a utility comparison.
& Only through the household budget and the price index; as silent on the valuation of public goods as the index above. \\
Final demand $c+g$\newline \scriptsize (CES aggregate)
& A CES quantity aggregate over household consumption and public absorption including the program, with baseline quantity weights and $\sigma=0.9$.
& The F2 and F3 program cells \emph{only}. It is \emph{not} the value ratio: for a basket that does not grow proportionally the two differ at first order and the sign can flip. $\sigma$ is the whole content of this row, not an irrelevance. Under F1 it is not reported: the preference tilt replaces the aggregator's own weights, so the index has no utility foundation and scores the intended reallocation as a loss.
& Values public absorption at cost, with unit weight. A stance rather than an implication: public demand is exogenous here, so nothing in the kernel makes it welfare-relevant. \\
Leisure-augmented\newline \scriptsize (first order)
& The bundle of the row above less the value of the additional work, $w\,\Delta N$, on the same scale. Parameter-free to first order, because the calibrated baseline equates the marginal disutility of labor with the wage; the curvature enters only at second order.
& The rows where employment absorbs the shock. The saving convention must be stated with it --- read as a leak or as deferred consumption --- because that choice, not the leisure preference, governs the sign at the wage-pinned pole.
& As the row above, plus the employment channel of a program that mobilizes labor. \\
